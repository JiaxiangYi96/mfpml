# Summary

The `mfpml` (multi-fidelity probabilistic machine learning) package provides a Python platform for implementing classic single- and multi-fidelity Bayesian machine learning surrogates and applying them to Bayesian optimization. Although numerous methods have been developed in the domain of multi-fidelity machine learning [@GiselleFernandezGodino2023] on regression purpose, no open-source software offers a comprehensive suite of tools for multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization. This package addresses this gap by providing a platform to replicate existing single- and multi-fidelity Bayesian methods based on Gaussian process regression. Build upon on this, we provide protocols for multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization that can actively optimize a black box problem with automatically adding new design of experiments from different fidelities considering its contribution, cost, and noise. It serves as a handy tool for developing new methods in the field of multi-fidelity probabilistic machine learning and Bayesian optimization.

# Statement of Need

`mfpml` is written in Python and depends on a few third-party packages, such as NumPy [@harris2020array] and SciPy [@2020SciPy-NMeth]. It includes detailed notebooks and autogenerated Sphinx documentation, allowing users to replicate existing methods and develop new ones with ease. Specifically, it provides essential modules for building machine learning models, including design of experiments, benchmark problems, models, and optimization.

Key features of `mfpml` include:

1. **Basic Methods**: Fundamental implementations of popular methods, such as Gaussian process regression [@Rasmussen2005], Co-Kriging [@Forrester2007], and corresponding extensions [@Han2012].
2. **Advanced Methods**: Advanced techniques for Bayesian optimization [@Jones1998], including single-fidelity and multi-fidelity optimization.
3. **Future Development**: Ongoing work includes adding constrained optimization and multi-objective optimization methods, which will be included in future versions.


Recently, probabilistic machine learning and the corresponding Bayesian optimization frameworks have received increasing attention, where various multi-fidelity probabilistic machine learning models [@Rasmussen2005;@Forrester2007;@Han2012] have been employed for different learning functions, such as MF-GP-UCB [@cheng2022enhanced], BOCA [@kandasamy2016multi], and multi-information-source optimization [@poloczek2016multiinformationsourceoptimization], among others [@peherstorfer2018survey;@li2024multi]. These studies have established a solid methodological foundation; however, they are typically presented as standalone algorithms rather than as part of a unified and extensible software framework. These methods are particularly important for real-world simulation- or experiment-driven problems, where it is common to have multiple data sources with different fidelities, i.e., a limited number of accurate high-fidelity data points and a large amount of biased low-fidelity data. Representative applications include aerodynamic and structural design optimization [@yi2025practicalmultifidelitymachinelearning], material modeling and discovery [@YI2026118479], and structural reliability analysis [@yi2022sbsc+,yi2021active], among others.

In a similar scope, several Python packages provide Gaussian process or Bayesian optimization functionality, such as `GPyTorch` [@gardner2021gpytorchblackboxmatrixmatrixgaussian], `BoTorch` [@balandat2020botorch], `Ax` [@olson2025ax], and `Emukit` [@emukit2023], primarily based on `PyTorch`. Moreover, `Trieste` [@trieste2023] provides multi-fidelity modeling workflows within a TensorFlow-based Bayesian optimization framework. However, these frameworks primarily target single- or multi-fidelity modeling or deep Gaussian processes and are often coupled with large software dependencies. The `SMT` toolbox [@saves2024smt] offers various surrogate models with limited capability to integrate multi-fidelity data, yet it lacks support for multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization. By contrast, `mfpml` is designed as a lightweight and standalone toolkit that focuses explicitly on multi-fidelity Gaussian process regression and the corresponding Bayesian optimization framework. It serves both as a platform for developing and benchmarking novel multi-fidelity Gaussian process methods and as an easily deployable tool for real-world applications such as aerodynamic shape optimization, materials design, and other simulation-driven engineering problems.
